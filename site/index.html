    Three layers, three different answers

    Confirmed

    A package can pass one layer and fail the next. Keep them separate in every conversation with a buyer.

    LayerQuestion it answersWho decides
    TechnicalDoes it disintegrate and biodegrade under defined lab conditions?ASTM D6400, D6868, D8410; EN 13432; OK compost HOME. Proven by a certifier (BPI, TÜV Austria, DIN CERTCO).
    LegalMay the word “compostable” go on the pack in this state?State labeling statutes (CA, WA, CO, MN, MD); FTC Green Guides elsewhere. California adds organic-input, PFAS and food-waste tests.
    OperationalWill the local composter accept it and break it down?Facility operators and local programs. Most decline compostable plastics today; field testing (CMA, CFTP) is how that changes.

    A claim attaches to the finished item. A compostable film with a conventional label, ink, adhesive, zipper or laminate layer fails as a unit, so confirm what the component is combined with before quoting any verdict here.

    State detail

    PFAS in food packaging

    Signaled

    A separate layer from the labeling statutes. Most state bans reach only plant-fiber packaging; a smaller group reaches food packaging of any material, which includes film. Every compostable claim in California also requires total organic fluorine under 100 ppm, and BPI certification requires no intentionally added fluorinated chemicals.

    StateEffectiveScopeNote

    Compiled from law-firm and trade trackers (BCLP; Packaura, Oct 2026; Packaged Sustainable, Apr 2026), which disagree on some scope details. Confirm against each statute before relying on it. Whether a seaweed-based film counts as “plant-based” or “plant fiber” under each definition is unverified.

    How EPR programs treat compostables

    Signaled

      Known gaps

        Maintenance

        Weekly review pass: CalRecycle (AB 1201, SB 54 list), USDA AMS and NOSB, Oregon DEQ, Washington Ecology, CDPHE, MPCA, CAA schedules and the dockets in the litigation table. Any changed figure gets a new evidence label and the page’s date moves. An entry not rechecked in 30 days should be treated as Signaled.

        Neutrality rules for any public version

        A public Atlas only works if it reads as a reference rather than a pitch: verdicts follow the cited text even where that cuts against compostables, competitor materials get the same treatment as Sway’s, no product names appear in verdicts, and corrections are logged. Publishing it, and every interpretation it would make public, is a company-positioning decision that sits with the CEO.
